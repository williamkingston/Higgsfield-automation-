# HETPA Compliance Checklist

**NY Real Property Law §265-a — Home Equity Theft Prevention Act**

This is a hard gate, not a nice-to-have. Run it before generating outreach copy, a contract draft, or advice to approach an owner.

## 1. Does it apply?

HETPA applies to a purchase from a homeowner whose **1–4 family owner-occupied residence** meets any of:

- [ ] Currently in foreclosure (a lis pendens/notice of pendency has been filed), **or**
- [ ] On a tax lien sale list, **or**
- [ ] Owner is 2+ months behind on the mortgage **and** the deal involves a reconveyance or repurchase option (e.g. sale-leaseback, equity-skimming structures)

If any box is checked, treat the deal as a **"covered contract"** for the rest of this checklist. If you're sourcing leads from the lis pendens pipeline or the tax lien sale list (see `data-sources.md`), assume HETPA applies by default — those two sources are covered by definition.

## 2. Required contract elements (if covered)

A HETPA-compliant contract must include:

- [ ] The purchaser's name, business address, and phone number
- [ ] The property address
- [ ] The total consideration to be paid
- [ ] Complete payment terms
- [ ] A **detachable Notice of Cancellation** form, in duplicate, printed in **12-point bold type**
- [ ] A **5 business day** right to cancel, during which the purchaser cannot transfer or encumber any interest in the property

## 3. Void/unenforceable clauses

The following are void if included in a covered contract — don't let outreach or contract drafts imply they're enforceable:

- [ ] Waivers of the seller's HETPA rights
- [ ] Limitations on the purchaser's liability
- [ ] Arbitration clauses (voidable at the seller's option — the seller can still choose to enforce them, but can't be forced into them)

## 4. Penalties for getting this wrong

- The NY Attorney General can seek injunctive relief.
- Up to **$25,000 per violation** — this is per violation, not per contract, so a contract missing multiple required elements compounds.

## 5. What to actually tell the user

If they haven't confirmed an attorney has drafted a HETPA-compliant contract package:

> Before any outreach goes out on a lis-pendens or tax-lien lead, you need an attorney-drafted HETPA-compliant contract package — this is a second template alongside your standard contract, not an amendment to it. Don't send mail, make calls, or door-knock on these leads until that's in hand.

Don't draft contract language yourself even if asked — this is exactly the kind of statutory boilerplate (disclosure formatting, cancellation notice) where a template built by an attorney licensed in NY is required, and getting the formatting or timing wrong (e.g. 12-point bold, exact 5-business-day window) is the actual failure mode HETPA punishes.

## 6. Non-covered leads

Probate, DOB/HPD violations (absent a lis pendens or tax lien), and absentee/long-tenure owner leads are **not** HETPA-covered by default — the statute is about foreclosure/tax-lien distress and reconveyance structures, not general off-market sourcing. Still recommend standard consumer-protection-conscious contract practices, but don't over-apply HETPA's specific disclosure/cancellation mechanics to non-covered deals; it isn't the applicable statute there and doing so just adds unnecessary friction to a deal that doesn't need it.
